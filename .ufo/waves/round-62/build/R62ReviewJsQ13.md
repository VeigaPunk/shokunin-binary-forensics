## F6 (MEDIUM) — a word-based "flags every verifier mention" check cannot separate a defect from a Q13-compliant entry

The round-62 no-credit check flags any line naming a `verify_*.py` script or using a coverage verb. A reference triage labeller is in scratch: `q13_mention_classifier.py` (REVIEW / OK / SKIP per claim block) and `test_q13_classifier.py`.

**Three earlier versions were unsafe and were rebuilt.** The critic ran three independent adversarial batteries against it. The first (18 cases) found 12 of 13 coverage claims mislabelled: disclaimer phrases and negators cancelled coverage verbs elsewhere in the same clause. The second (16 cases) found 13 of 16 still mislabelled, because the verb axis was wrong — a coverage claim can put the value before the verb ("All three names are guarded"), use a verb outside any list ("rejected by", "fails", "escapes", "relies on", "safe under"), use a noun instead of a verb ("Coverage: …"), or split verb and object with a comma. The third (12 cases) found 8 of 12 still mislabelled plus a false positive, and showed that changing which words the labeller looks for moves the misses rather than removing them.

The working version drops the verb axis and detects coverage on three relations instead: **(A)** a named check and a value co-occur in one clause with no denial scoped to that value; **(B)** a coverage predicate applied directly to a value; **(C)** a check artifact in a covering relation to its object ("covers them", "pinned by", "enforced by"), including across a sentence boundary. A denial is scoped only when it is adjacent to the value it governs and is a *property* denial; a quantified-scope denial flips the sense ("no name escapes X" asserts coverage of every name), and a double negation does too ("the names are not unguarded").

**Measured rates, not a zero-miss claim:** 18/18 on the first battery, 16/16 on the second, 12/12 with 0 false positives on the third, 41/41 on my own, and the frozen corpus sweep is **REVIEW=10 / OK=180 / SKIP=764**. The accepted C09 posture classifies OK. These rates are measured against batteries written by a non-author; each round's misses came from wording neither of us had written yet, so the true error rate on unseen wording is higher than these figures, and the label must not be read as a guarantee.

**The labeller ranks; it does not decide.** A false positive costs a little reviewer attention, a false negative costs a missed defect, so the design is asymmetric toward REVIEW.
